For a 17-year-old, BMI is read against boys of the same age (CDC growth chart). 21.8 sits around the 59th percentile. The healthy zone is the 5th to 85th percentile, so you are right in the middle. BMI cannot tell muscle from fat, so we also track your waist.

Goal

Build lean muscleGain about 3 to 4 kg in 13 weeks, mostly muscle. That is 250 to 350 g a week.
Keep body fat lowEat a small surplus of about 200 to 300 kcal, not a big "bulk". Waist should stay almost the same.
Hit every muscle twice a weekPush, Pull, Legs, then again with different angles. Each part of each muscle is trained 2 times.
Learn perfect form firstNo injuries, no ego lifting. Strength comes fast when the form is clean.

Food rules

Weigh chicken raw100 g raw boneless chicken gives about 22 g protein. Cooked, it shrinks to about 70 g.
Oil and ghee count1 tsp = 45 kcal. Use about 1 tsp per curry serving. Deep-fried food only once a week.
Junk foodOnce a week is fine. Daily chips, cold drinks and sweets will add fat, not muscle.
No supplements neededReal food covers everything. Doctors (American Academy of Pediatrics) advise teens under 18 not to use creatine, fat burners or pre-workouts. Pintola oats are food, so they are fine.

3-month plan

13 weeks. Every month gets a little harder.

Weekly check-in

Weigh yourself every morning after the toilet, before food. Only the weekly average matters, because daily weight jumps around with water and food.

How to adjust food

Weekly average changeWhat to do
Less than +0.2 kg, 2 weeks in a rowAdd about 150 kcal a day: 1 roti, or 1 banana, or 1 tbsp peanut butter
+0.2 to +0.4 kgPerfect. Change nothing
More than +0.5 kg, 2 weeks in a rowRemove about 150 kcal a day: 1 roti less, or half the rice at dinner
Waist grows more than 1 cm in a monthToo much fat gain. Remove 150 kcal a day

Measure your waist at the belly button every 2 weeks. Take a front and side photo on the 1st of each month in the same light.

Where this comes from

  • NSCA youth resistance training position statement (Faigenbaum et al., 2009): supervised training is safe and helps teens when technique comes first.
  • Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger (2017), weekly sets vs muscle growth: more weekly sets give more growth, about 10 or more sets per muscle per week works well. Training a muscle 2 times a week beat 1 time (Schoenfeld et al., 2016).
  • Schoenfeld et al. (2017), low vs high load: muscle grows in a wide rep range when sets are taken close to failure.
  • Adolescent athlete protein reviews: 1.2 to 2.0 g per kg is the usual range, spread over 4 to 5 meals of about 0.3 g per kg.
  • Iraki et al. (2019), off-season nutrition for natural lifters: small surplus and slow weight gain keep fat gain low.
  • American Academy of Sleep Medicine (2016): teens 13 to 18 need 8 to 10 hours of sleep.
  • American Academy of Pediatrics: performance supplements such as creatine are not advised under 18.
  • Food values: USDA FoodData Central and Indian Food Composition Tables (IFCT 2017). Pintola oats: brand label, about 24 g protein per 100 g. Check your pack.
